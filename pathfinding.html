pathfinding

Drag to draw walls, then race BFS, DFS, Dijkstra, or A* from the filled cell to the outlined one. Dimmer cells were explored; the brightest trail is the final path.

how it works

All four algorithms explore the grid outward from the start cell, one "frontier" of cells at a time. What differs is which cell they pick next and what order they leave the frontier:

  • BFS keeps a plain queue (first in, first out). It explores every cell 1 step away before any cell 2 steps away, so the first time it reaches the end, that path is guaranteed shortest — as long as every step costs the same.
  • DFS keeps a stack (last in, first out) instead. It commits to one direction and follows it as far as possible before backtracking, so it finds *a* path, not necessarily the shortest one.
  • Dijkstra keeps a priority queue ordered by total distance-so-far. It always expands whichever known cell is currently cheapest to reach, which is what lets it handle different step costs correctly.
  • A* is Dijkstra plus a guess: it prioritizes cells by distance-so-far + estimated distance remaining (straight-line distance to the end), so it explores toward the goal instead of in every direction equally.

Walls just get removed from the list of valid neighbors — no algorithm treats them specially beyond "can't step there."
